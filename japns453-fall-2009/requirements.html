Attendance & Participation 50%

Attendance & Preparation 20%

Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Students are expected to complete all of the readings for a week prior to the Monday course meetings.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding this requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

In order to receive credit for attendance, you must not only be present in class, but also bring marked copies of the readings to class and be prepared to give your opinions and questions on the reading. You must also bring any written material assigned for that class meeting.

SCREENINGs: Students are expected to view all course films during the Monday sessions. Students unable to attend a course meeting should arrange to borrow the DVD from the professor. Some films are also available at the library.

PARTICIPATION: In order to receive credit for in-class discussion, you must email me a self-assessment report in English. Every month students must email me a self-evaluation report assessing their own in-class performance. The self-assessment should include the words “self-assessment” in the subject header and a letter grade of A, B, C, D, or F. Due dates are posted on the schedule.

QUIZZES: If students are not coming to class prepared to participate, there may be pop quizzes that factor into the grade for preparation. There will be one scheduled quiz on the Japanese Film terms. The total value of all quizzes will be 10% of the final grade or 1/2 of the attendance and preparation grade.

In-Class Presentation 20%

The presentation will consist of a research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. One purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some contexts to a given week’s film which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings. Another is to introduce new vocabulary and grammar to students that relates to the content of the presentation. Students will also make interpretive readings of particular scenes from films during their presentations that relate the context to the film. As a companion to the presentations, WIKIs (consisting of visual aids, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required. See below.

All groups must plan a meeting with the instructor to discuss their plan and work at least two weeks prior to their presentation.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

For more detail, see presentation guidelines.

Regular Homework and In-class assignments 10%

Short homework assignments based on class discussions and film clips and brief in-class exercises will count towards participation.

Write 10 film journals for 35% of the grade.

Film Journal

Short (one page) reports on films viewed in class will be due within one week of their screening at the beginning of following week’s meeting. Students may choose which ten films they would like to write about, though it is strongly recommended that they begin with the Journals early in the semester. Journals shall be written or typed in JAPANESE. Entries must note the following:

  1. Film name and year of release
  2. Director’s name
  3. Production company name
  4. Setting
  5. Main characters’ names and brief descriptions (actors names if important)
  6. Plot summary
  7. Themes, motifs, recurring images

Notable scenes

Most importantly, thoughts on the film (between five and fifteen sentences.) These can be comments, criticisms, or questions. They should relate the film to class discussion and/or the readings. Journals are not to be summaries. See Film Journal Guidelines on course website for more details.

Final Paper 15%

Students will write a 2 to 3 page film review in Japanese. Students should base the structure of their reviews on the models we read and write for class. They should make reference to at least one other course film and one print source in Japanese.
