Being There – 40 points

Coming to class prepared is the easiest way to make the course interesting for you and for your classmates. Do it!

Class Attendance and Participation (10 points)

The collective experience of the class requires your regular attendance and productive participation in class discussion and group projects.

Students are expected to attend all class meetings and to complete all of a given week’s readings before class on Tuesday. Students should contact me if there are extenuating circumstances preventing their attendance.

Coming to class prepared and ready to participate means having completed the reading, having your text with you, marked with questions and concerns, having written down keywords for the readings of the day, and having identified important passages and puzzling ones. You only count as being in class if you’ve accomplished all of these things.

Attendance is quantifiable: grade reductions (1 for each missed class) begin after unexcused absence from 3 classes (excused absences are for scheduled university activities and religious observance, and we must have notice of these in advance; illness or emergencies are not excused, but you have the three “freebies”); significant tardiness counts as half an absence.

E-devices: computers, tablets, and cell phones are permitted as long as students are using them for class work such as note taking or replying to polls in class. Students will be asked to turn off devices or leave if they are being used for other functions and will receive no credit for attendance that day. No recording of any kind of class lectures or discussions is permitted. Take notes! It works your brain to stay focussed and process the material.

Discussion Board Postings (10 points)

Students are expected to participate in weekly online discussions on the course’s CANVAS website, discussing their thoughts on the readings and class discussions. Here you will be free to discuss topics that do not get raised in class or in the readings. Also, these discussions can form the basis for ideas for the papers.

Topics and the manner of the discussion will be decided by discussion leaders. Each student will take two turns being an online-discussion leader. A given week’s discussion leader(s) must write and post at least two paragraphs about the following week’s material by the prior Saturday at midnight. All students must sign up for a week to be a discussion leader by the third week of class.

Discussants (that is, everyone else for that week) must write at least one response to at least one of the discussion leaders by the following Monday at midnight. Responses may be any length five sentences or over. They can respond to any one or all of the discussion leaders for a given week.

Students must participate in discussion boards for ten of the 15 weeks of class (2 postings as discussion leaders and 8 as responders). If you participate in more then 10 weeks of discussion boards you will get BONUS points for each week you contribute to discussions over the minimum of 10 weeks requirements. So students may receive up to 5 BONUS points for extra work in discussion boards.

Posts for both leaders and discussants are expected to engage with and cite readings, class discussions, and other related material.

In-class Presentation (20 points)

Groups should plan meetings first with Darwin Tsen and then with Dr. Abel to discuss their topic at least one week prior to their presentation.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

The presentation will consist of a small research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on the historical contexts to a given week’s banned text or on topics in banned literature which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings. Handouts and web postings of information (consisting of visual aids, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required.

Presentations should not exceed 15 minutes in length.

Written Work – 60 points

All WRITTEN WORK is to be submitted to the appropriate dropbox on CANVAS in .doc, .docx., .pages, or .pdf format.

All papers are individual labors and therefore presenting the work of another as your own will result in a zero for the assignment or failure for the course.

All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) format.

Short argument: “Against Censorship” (5%)

Due Oct 11

1-2 pages double spaced. 5%

Mid-term Paper 1 (15%)

Due Sept. 29

Short paper discussing one of the works covered in the first third of the semester. Must include work on both banned texts and the historical contexts of their banning. 3-5 pages double-spaced. 15%

Short argument: “For Censorship” (5%)

Due Oct 25

1-2 pages double spaced. 5%

Mid-term Paper 2 (15%)

Due Nov 15

Short paper discussing one of the books covered in the second third of the semester. Must include work on both banned texts and the historical contexts of their banning. 3-5 pages double-spaced. 15%

Final Paper (20%)

Due Dec 13

Comparative research paper on the banning of two books from different times and places.

All students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing.

This research paper should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least two works based on your research about their historical production or reception, a discussion of how the works relate to course readings and sources not used in class.

7-10 pages double-spaced (excluding bibliography). 20%
