Participation in the seminar

Attendance and participation (20 points)

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you will be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply being present in class is not enough.

16–20
Frequent, substantial, and consistent verbal participation that demonstrates a good grasp of the reading. This includes answering and asking questions as well as taking part in group activities.
11–15
Occasional, inconsistent, or only superficial verbal participation. This includes demonstrating some grasp of the reading but failing to contribute to class discussion and/or group activities.
1–10
Infrequent, inconsistent, or superficial verbal participation. This includes rarely if ever answering or asking questions, failure to grasp the reading or participate in group activities.
0
Very poor or no participation; weak or no grasp of the readings; disruptive, disengaged, or uncooperative behavior.

Productive participation is qualitative, but comes down to preparing the assigned readings on time to discuss them in class, and then contributing to discussion. Discussion is more than giving your input, and includes responding to other speakers. Helping another student develop his/her ideas by making useful suggestions or asking questions can be the most productive form of participation.

This course is both student centered and learning centered. That is, it shifts the focus away from a model of knowledge imparted by professors (although inevitably there will be some of this), and toward analysis generated by the students. Research shows that students learn more — in terms of both skills and content — and retain more from this model of education. This model requires a greater commitment of time and energy from students. Enrollment in this class constitutes a commitment to put in that energy and to learn from one another.

Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Students are expected to complete all of the readings listed for the day in time for the beginning of class. If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

To receive credit for participation you must be prepared for class. Preparation is more than simply reading: take notes on the reading to prepare for discussion: write down questions you have about the texts, as well as points you think are interesting and things you disagree with. Contribute your thoughts to discussion. Students must bring a copy of assigned readings to class so we can refer to specific parts of the text: books, print-out, a tablet or laptop are all ways to do this. You should also have written notes, questions, and comments on your reading to prepare for discussion. Do not let your means of bringing the text to class become a distraction for others.

You are allowed up to four (4) absences in this course. For every class missed beyond the allowed four, you will lose 5 points from the “Attendance and Participation” portion of your grade. After nine unexcused absences, you will fail the course. Late work will not be accepted in cases of unexcused absences. You are expected to arrive on time for class; recurrent tardiness may result in a recorded absence. Failing to complete reading or do other course preparation for class meeting will also be recorded as a participation failure for the day.

Art+Reading Posts (10 points)

This is a ten part assignment nearly weekly assignment.

For this assignment students are expected to post 10 Art+Reading Posts to the weekly discussion boards by the end of the semester.

For this assignment you will:

  • choose a work of Japanese art mentioned in the reading for the week or adjacent to the reading.
  • Title your discussion board post with the Name of the Author, Work of Art Title (Year)
  • Post a page number from our reading in which the work appears or adjacent material appears. In addition, if possible provide an internet link, image, or video of the work.
  • Post what you find interesting and worthy of note about the work of art.
  • Post what you think of the way the reading treats it. Does the reading do it justice or miss something?
  • Post about how the work relates to another Japanese cultural product with which you are familiar such as a novel, film, fashion style, favorite manga or anime?
  • Be prepared to speak about your post in class if called upon to do so.

These are opportunities for you to display what you have garnered from the readings and to influence class discussions. Students who have difficulty speaking in class are particularly encouraged to take this assignment seriously as it will prepare you to be more outspoken in class and show your classmates and instructor that you have important ideas. Selected comments and questions from the posts will be put up on the slides to open class discussion.

The posts will be shared with the entire class and students should not consider them confidential work for the eyes of the professor only.

Posts on a given class’s readings should be submitted digitally by the midnight before the class’s meeting for which the reading is due (for example, post for Monday’s class on Sunday at midnight to Canvas.)

Students may post up to 5 extra of these assignments for up to 5 bonus points total (at one point each).

Contextual Presentation (10 points)

The presentation will consist of a small research (outside and beyond given course materials) project and in-class oral/visual presentation. The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some historical, material, financial, geographical, biological, biographical, cultural or other contexts to a given week’s text or artistic media which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class discussions or readings. Handouts or slides (consisting of visual aids, quotations, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

You must have your presentation pre-approved before presentation. Drop by office hours either in person or virtually for this approval.

You must submit to Professor Abel a copy of your slides and/or handouts prior to your pre approval meeting.

It is best to drop by early in your process in case your presentation is not approved and also because Dr. Abel can give you help in figuring out how to research the thing you have chosen. The meeting is to facilitate you arriving at a presentation that is both of interest to you and beneficial to the class.

Presentations should not exceed ten minutes in length. Presentations will generally happen in Wednesday class sessions.

Written assignments

Special Research Project (15 points)

Art Thing Explainer-Thing Maker

This is a group research and making project. Groups will decide a topic and individual students within a group will choose a single work of art, product, or daily use item related to topic and the materials we have been discussing in class.

They will research the background of the material. They will try to create something inspired by the thing and their research. And then present on the thing and what they’ve made to class in the final weeks of the semester.

All projects to be explained must be approved by Professor Abel before Spring Break. This project is to be decided by the student and approved by Professor Abel.

There are three parts to this assignment—research, making, presenting.

The idea behind it is to allow students use their research to inform their creativity and then to reflect on how the different modes (research and experimental making) have informed their understanding of the art/thing they have chose.

It is a chance to think beyond the scope of the traditional research paper. Possible successful projects examples include researching and designing a series of kimono patterns, researching and retelling Japanese fairy tales in manga format, researching and distilling a lengthy manga into a poster of your design, researching and creating a video of scene from one of the things we’ve read, or researching and reframing stories we have read in class using alternative different images (from your own design or taken from elsewhere), record yourself doing a work of performance art in the HUB inspired by one of the artists we discuss.

The possibilities are limited only by your imagination and by your ability to convince Professor Abel that this is a worthwhile, stimulating, and intellectual endeavor that will get you thinking about Japanese visual cultures in a way which you would otherwise have been unable to achieve.

SPECIAL PROJECTS Presentations should include reflection on your process of creation. Presentations should address what was learned through the process of making that could not have been achieved through simply research or reading about the art. You need to submit a lasting version of your presentation to be evaluated on the day of your presentation (this can be in the form of a link to a website of your design, a printed handout, a powerpoint file, or whatever means you think appropriate to the project that best represents it).

One half of the grade 7.5 points is for the groupwork, the synthetic work that coordinates the projects. And one half of the grade 7.5 points is for the individual work.

Papers

Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing. Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one visual and one textual work discussed in class, a discussion of how the works relate to several of the course themes and readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class.

Students must use at least one academic journal article, one book published by a university press, and a primary source as references for their research. Professor Abel can help you with finding such resources during office hours. Librarians also can help you with this task.

The most important quality of each paper is that it be organized around a clear thesis. This means 1) papers must have an introduction stating the thesis, 2) every paragraph must have a topic sentence connected to the thesis, and 3) the conclusion must assert the significance of the thesis. Check each paper to make sure it does these three things.

Papers are graded on a rubric with two areas. Material familiarity: identifying appropriate primary material in visual studies; performing a close reading of that primary material; synthesizing classroom learning with individual research; and identifying appropriate secondary material in visual studies. Argumentation: a strong, arguable, and supportable thesis (does the paper have a point?); clear supporting evidence (examples from the primary text and contextual research); and a sound conclusion that flows from the argument and evidence. Each criterion is rated Excellent, Adequate, Poor, or None/Fails to meet.

All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) style or Chicago style formats. Where papers quote readings, these quotations must be cited correctly cite (using complete MLA or Chicago style – no made-up or mix’n’match formats!). Students are strongly encouraged to use the Writing Center (information at www.psu.edu/dept/cew/) and to consult with the professor while preparing their papers

Bonus opportunities

In any semester, each and everyone of us may have issues arise that prevent us from working at the top of our abilities. The evaluation of your progress in this course acknowledges that occasionally life gets in the way of work.

To that end, potential optional bonus assignments will be announced in class, via email, and/or on the website. These often will be in the form of visiting a talk, performance, or exhibit on or around campus, writing a short reflection piece on your experience, then submitting it for evaluation.

In addition, Dr. Abel is willing to consider proposals for potentially relevant and appropriate bonus projects (such as writing an extra research paper for 10 points, doing an extra class presentation for up to 10 points, or traveling to a museum and reporting on their Japanese visual art collection over spring break up to 10 points).
