Course description

The visual, tactile, and literary arts play key roles in how people make sense of themselves and the world. Japan provides one key example through which to observe the use and function of art to create a sense of relation of the individual to society. The arts can construct not only the image and identity of a nation and national culture, but also the image and identity of other national cultures. In recent years, Japanese popular culture has been reborn around the world. A global generation has grown up watching anime and reading manga in Spanish, Chinese, Russian, and English. This course “Beyond Anime” is designed to contextualize the recent appropriation and dissemination of Japanese popular culture through the cyclical history of such appropriations through the modern period. Through this context, the course will explore the precursors, antecedents, and contexts to our present cultural moment. Through film, photography, posters, matchbook-labels, textiles, industrial design, stories, and myriad other popular media, this seminar-style study of Japanese popular visual culture will help students see Japanese visual arts in terms that are local to Japanese aesthetics and through those that transcend local cultures. As such, this course confronts the exoticist notion that Japan’s arts have always placed a disproportionately heavy emphasis on the visual. Through comparative readings of cultural and visual material from Japan, this course will raise questions of aesthetic, cultural, and national difference. Specific topics will vary with instructor, but may include: the rendering of three dimensional space through perspective, the use of pictures in the service of narrative versus the use of pictures as narrative, and how notions of negative space promise deep insight and risk crass stereotypes.

Objectives

  • Students will explore the rich cultural diversity of popular visual arts and narrative from Japan. Reading through various cultures of visuality, the exposure to cultural material, artifacts, and objects from Japan within their historical, social, and aesthetic contexts will expand understanding not only of Japan but of how to learn about other cultures.
  • Students will gain a deeper understanding of the importance of visuality in communication for aesthetics, literature, and politics.
  • Students will develop critical thinking in speech and writing, and develop writing analytic skills appropriate to their various class projects.
  • Students will interrogate and engage with problems of cultural difference in ways of consuming art and viewing the world.
  • Students will hone their skills at identifying their own cultural assumptions and how they interpret the world around them.
  • Gain a sophisticated knowledge of national cultural initiatives that have helped shape the world we live.
  • Understand their own role in these broad international movements.
  • Refine their skills for the effective communication of their knowledge by researching and by presenting their project in both written and oral form.
  • Learn the context for popular culture and its relation to the avant garde arts
