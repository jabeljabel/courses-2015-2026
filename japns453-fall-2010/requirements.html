Coursework requirements

Films

As a portion of your homework each week you are REQUIRED to watch a film on reserve at the library or available freely over the internet. For your convenience an OPTIONAL screening time for the class has been set for MONDAY EVENINGS 3-6PM. RM 214W. PATEE Library. 2nd Floor. Capacity 15. (Questions call (814) 865-3616)

IF YOU OPT NOT TO WATCH THE FILM DURING THE SCREENING TIME, YOU ARE STILL RESPONSIBLE TO WATCH THE FILM ON RESERVE OR ON YOUR OWN BEFORE TUESDAY’s CLASS.

All DVDs are on reserve in the Media Center in the Library.

Readings

You are also responsible for completing the week’s readings prior to class. The instructor reserves the right to give pop quizzes on the readings if students seem not to be keeping up with the assignments.

Most readings will be provided via links from the course website. Students should bring copies of readings to class.

Short assignments 10%

5 short homework assignments based on class discussions and film clips and 5 brief in-class exercises will count towards participation.

The 5 homework will be assigned on Thursday classes meetings to be due on the following Tuesday meeting. See individual assignments for instructions.

Presentations 20%

2 Presentations will consist of a research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. One purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some contexts to a given week’s film which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings. Another is to introduce new vocabulary and grammar to students that relates to the content of the presentation. Students will also make interpretive readings of particular scenes from films during their presentations that relate the context to the film. As a companion to the presentations, WIKIs (consisting of visual aids, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required. See below.

All students must plan a meeting with the instructor to discuss their plan and work at least two weeks prior to their presentation.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

For more detail, see presentation guidelines.

Wiki 15%

Wikis will serve as a lasting and changing testament to your work. See Wiki Guidelines for more.

Option one

Write 5 film journals to equal 20% and a final paper to account for 15%

Film Journal 20%

Short (one page) reports on films viewed in class will be due within one week of their screening at the beginning of following week’s Tuesday meeting. Students may choose which ten films they would like to write about, though it is strongly recommended that they begin with the Journals early in the semester. Journals shall be written or typed in JAPANESE. Entries must note the following:

  1. Film name and year of release
  2. Director’s name
  3. Production company name
  4. Setting
  5. Main characters’ names and brief descriptions (actors names if important)
  6. Plot summary
  7. Themes, motifs, recurring images, and notable scenes

Most importantly, thoughts on the film (between five and fifteen sentences.) These can be comments, criticisms, or questions. They should relate the film to class discussion and/or the readings. Journals are not to be summaries. See Film Journal Guidelines on course website for more details.

Final Paper 15%

Students will write a 3-5 page film review in Japanese. Students should base the structure of their reviews on the models we read and write for class. They should make reference to at least one other course film and one print source in Japanese.

Due DUE MONDAY DEC 13.

Please note submit all written work via Angel email.

Option two

Final project 35% of the grade.

This will be a substantial final project, proposed by the student to be approved by the professor. Possibilities include: a lengthy (20-25 page) research paper using Japanese language research; subtitling a previously unsubtitled and fairuse film in Japanese; and reshooting scenes from a film we have seen in class using the same dialogue to illustrate, emphasize, or improve on film techniques used in the original.

Proposals for projects must be submitted for review by the fifth week of class.

Attendance & Preparation 20%

Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Students are expected to complete all of the readings and screenings for a week prior to the Tuesday course meetings.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding this requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

In order to receive credit for attendance, you must not only be present in class, but also bring marked copies of the readings to class and be prepared to give your opinions and questions on the reading. You must also bring any written material assigned for that class meeting.

SCREENINGs: Students are expected to view a given week’s film prior to our Tuesday meetings. Students unable to attend the screenings should arrange to borrow the DVD from reserves (see “FILMS” above.)

PARTICIPATION: In order to receive credit for in-class discussion, you must email me a self-assessment report in English. Every month students must email me a self-evaluation report assessing their own in-class performance. The self-assessment should include the words “self-assessment” in the subject header and a letter grade of A, B, C, D, or F. Due dates are posted on the schedule.

QUIZZES: If students are not coming to class prepared to participate, there may be pop quizzes that factor into the grade for preparation. There will be one scheduled quiz on the Japanese Film terms. The total value of all quizzes will be 10% of the final grade or 1/2 of the attendance and preparation grade.

Evaluation guidelines for attendance & participation

90–100 %

80–89 %

70–79 %
Student reluctantly participates in class activities and is often unprepared; rarely practices critical thinking; does not demonstrate sufficient knowledge of course materials.
60–69 %
Student is unwilling to participate in class activities and unable to ask or answer questions; is generally unprepared; demonstrates little knowledge of course materials.
0–59 %
Student is absent (physically or mentally), unprepared, or disruptive in class.
