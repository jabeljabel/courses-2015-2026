Participation

Attendance and participation (20%)

A portion of the grade for this course is directly tied to your participation in this class. Successful participation is defined as consistently adhering to University requirements in addition to those outlined here. Participation includes engaging in class, group, or other activities that solicit your feedback on the readings or material.

Students are expected to complete all of the reading for the week in time for class. Students are expected to attend all class meetings. If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you should be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply turning up is not enough.

Attendance and participation rubric (20 points):

16–20
Frequent, substantial, and consistent verbal participation that demonstrates a good grasp of the reading. This includes answering and asking questions as well as taking part in group activities.
11–15
Occasional, inconsistent, or only superficial verbal participation. This includes demonstrating some grasp of the reading but failing to contribute to class discussion and/or group activities.
1–10
Infrequent, inconsistent, or superficial verbal participation. This includes rarely if ever answering or asking questions, failure to grasp the reading or participate in group activities.
0
Very poor or no participation; weak or no grasp of the readings; disruptive, disengaged, or uncooperative behavior.

Regular attendance is required. You are allowed six absences free of consequence. Additional unexcused absences will result in your class participation grade being lowered a letter grade for each additional absence. After nine unexcused absences, you will fail the course.

To be counted as present for attendance, you must be prepared and participate in that class session. Preparation of class materials and the frequency and quality of participation in discussions is part of showing up.

Late work will not be accepted in cases of unexcused absences.

You are expected to arrive on time for class; recurrent or extreme tardiness may result in a recorded absence.

At two points (weeks 5, 10) in the semester, I will post mid-term evaluations of each student’s participation and attendance.

In-class presentations (20%)

There are two presentations for this class:

  1. on your research project, related to a particular week’s readings;
  2. on your special project, a creative project relating to the material of our course study.

The presentation of research will consist of a small research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. The purpose of these presentations is to educate the class on some historical, material, financial, geographical, biological, biographical, or other contexts to a given week’s text or on topics which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings. Slides (consisting of visual aids, quotations, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required. You must submit to professor Abel a copy of your slides and/or handouts.

Research presentations should not exceed ten minutes in length. Presentations will generally happen on Wednesday’s class.

All presenters should plan a meeting with Professor Abel in office hours to discuss their topic at least one week prior to their presentation. The meeting is to facilitate you arriving at a topic that is both of interest to you and beneficial to the class.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the fourth week of class.

Special project presentations will occur in the final week of class. For details on the project see the special project description below. Presentations should range from 10-20 minutes, discussing the concept for the project and what was learned from making it that could not have been otherwise learned.

Presentations rubric: Presentations are assessed in three categories of substance: 1) the quality of the content presented (does the work represent the standards of work we discuss in class?); 2) the form of the presentation (was it energetically and clearly presented?); the contribution to and engagement with ideas raised in readings and class discusssions (does the presentation add to our ongoing studies?)

Written work

Special project (15%)

This project is to be decided by the student and approved by Professor Abel. The idea behind it is to allow students to think beyond the scope of the traditional research paper. Possible successful projects examples include retelling a fairy tales in graphic novel format, translating scenes from an as yet untranslated graphic novel, reframing stories we have read in class using alternative different images (from your own design or taken from elsewhere), and tellings of new stories using a mash-up of images from graphic novels in our course. But the possibilities are limited only by your imagination and by your ability to convince Professor Abel that this is a worthwhile, stimulating, creative, and intellectual endeavor that will get you thinking about graphic novels in a way which you would otherwise might have been unable to achieve.

SPECIAL PROJECTS will be presented on in the final weeks of class. Sign up for your date before Thanksgiving break by emailing Professor Abel. Your project must be complete by the day you present it.

Make sure to clear your topic with Professor Abel in office hours at least two weeks prior to presenting your special project to the class.

Research

Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing. Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one literary work discussed in class, a discussion of how the work relates to several of the course readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class. All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) style format. Students are encouraged to use academic journals, books published by university presses, and primary sources as references for their research.

Mid-term paper (20%)

The finished product should be between three and five (excluding bibliography).

Due October 2

Final paper (25%)

The finished product should be between seven and ten pages (excluding bibliography).

Due December 14
