Course description

This survey course in modern Japanese literature (1840s-1990s) will examine the relationship between and coexistence of war and modern literature. Scholars have made the case that from the time of its first modern war in the mid-1890s through end of the Asia-Pacific War in 1945, Japan was either at war or preparing for war. In addition, contemporary Japan is still often referred to as “postwar.” In the wake of catastrophic violence, then Japanese prose and poetry flourished.

To what extent was this coexistence of war and literature a symbiotic one? How does modern Japanese literature look through the lens of war? How does war look through the lens of literature? How did war damage literature? How did literature damage war? How was literature born from war? What kind of war is birthed in literature? These are some of the questions this course hopes to raise. By examining the technologies of dissemination and control that have made both war and literature arguably more accessible and supposedly less meaningful over the years, this course will encourage students to find connections between distant texts from the past and current, more local events.
