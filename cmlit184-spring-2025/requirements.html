Attendance and Participation (20 points)

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you will be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply being present in class is not enough to count.

16–20
Frequent, substantial, and consistent verbal participation that demonstrates a good grasp of the reading. This includes answering and asking questions as well as taking part in group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are well prepared, as demonstrated by your comments made in class. You are interested in and respect others’ opinions. You complete the readings before class and during class you respond to questions, demonstrate knowledge of course materials, and foster a vibrant intellectual community.
11–15
Occasional, inconsistent, or only superficial verbal participation. This includes demonstrating some grasp of the reading but failing to contribute to class discussion and/or group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are generally prepared, and you make an effort to refer to readings and course topics. You are willing to participate in discussions in class. You might be occassionally distracted but you do not distract others.
1–10
Infrequent, inconsistent, or superficial verbal participation. This includes rarely if ever answering or asking questions, failure to grasp the reading or participate in group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You reluctantly participate in class activities, and you are often unprepared. You only have skimmed the reading. You rarely practice critical thinking and do not demonstrate sufficient knowledge of course materials. You are consistently distracted and/or distract others.
0
Very poor or no participation; weak or no grasp of the readings; disruptive, disengaged, or uncooperative behavior. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are not willing to participate in class activities. You have not done the reading before class. You are unable to ask or answer questions. You are generally unprepared and demonstrate little knowledge of course materials. You are consistently distracted and/or distract others. You are absent (physically or mentally), unprepared, or disruptive in class.

You are allowed up to four (4) absences in this course. For every class missed beyond the allowed four, you will lose 5 points from the “Attendance and Participation” portion of your grade.

Making Stories/Telling Stories (10 points)

This is a group activity. Once during the semester your group will meet in class to create a story inspired by our readings. You will work in class together to write a very short story. At the end of the class workshop session all stories will be submitted. Then a few weeks later your group will present (this can be a reading, performance, slide show or video) your story to the class. All members of the group will receive the same grade for the assignment.

2 MidTerm Exams (each exam is worth 15 points. so a total of 30 points for both exams)

The two mid-term exams are three-part events of 5 points each.

Review session: On the Friday 10 days before the exams begin, we have review sessions. During the review session, the class determines 30 of the most salient terms from the readings and our discussions for the exam. Students also propose and refine potential essay questions that link three or more stories and at least one critical or theoretical work.

Part 1. (5 points). On Monday of exam week, students work individually. They are given a list of 20 of the 30 terms. Students individually must choose 10 to write short essays listing the identifications (displaying that they understand the meaning, definitions, contexts, and functions of the terms) and significance (why they are important for the understanding literature, the class, culture, society). Students are allowed to bring a single sheet of notes on US letter size paper to use during the exam.

Part 2. (5 points). On Wednesday of exam week, students work in their group to discuss the 10 terms left off of Monday’s exam. They then choose 4 of these 10 terms in addition to choosing 1 term from the 20 on Monday’s exam. They then spend the class session writing up to write up IDs and significances for the 5 terms they have chosen. Every group member of a given group receives the same grade for this assignment.

Part 3. (5 points). On Friday of exam week, students work individually. This is a two part essay exam. For Essay 1: students are given 2 essay questions from the pool of questions developed in the review session from which they must choose to answer one question. For Essay 2: Professor Abel will create two questions based around the terms leftover from Wednesday’s session; students must choose one of these to answer. During the class seession, students choose 2 of the 4 to answer.

Group Presentation (10 points)

This 5 minute group presentation in which your will pick a claim made in one of our critical or theoretical readings. Your group will evaluate the claim through your reading of two or more short stories on the syllabus (as long as your presentation fulfills this minimal requirement, you may also include other stories from beyond the class syllabus such as those in popular films or television shows). You will define and analyze the claim, and then use the content of the short stories to either validate or invalidate the claim. Your group will sign up for the presentation by week three of the course. Group presentations will generally happen on Wednesdays and Fridays.

Comparative Essay (5 points each/ 10 total)

Two short essays will be written in class this semester to build your skills of close reading, comparing and contrasting, and forming arguments. The assignment will be announced on a Monday and you will be given twenty minutes to write on that week’s Wednesday. Students may bring a 3x5 index card for notes to the Wednesday session. Each writing assignment is worth 5 points.

Comparative Essay 1 for Wednesday February 5th’s class

Writing Prompt:

Using evidence from at least two short stories we’ve read in class (our primary sources), analyze and discuss claims from at least two critical, theoretical, or interpretive essays (our secondary sources). Project (20 points)

The purpose of this final project is to allow students to display a synthesis of what they have learned in this class as well as outside research on their topic. Students can choose from one of two options below or propose their own.

Options

  • Rework a short story that we have read in light of one or more other short story and one or more critical / theoretical reading. This is a creative endeavor. You can rewrite a story from scratch or play mad-libs with it changing words and phrases in each sentence to create something new and completely different. You can translate a story into a poem or graphic novel. You can create a short video of the story. You can try to do a mashup of several stories. You will then write a reflection of 1-2 pages explaining what you did and why you did it.
  • Comparative research essay. Choose two or more short stories from class and two or more critical/theoretical pieces that help you understand the stories. Then write a 5-7 page essay arguing why the stories you have chosen are important examples of or aberrations from the works of criticism you have chosen.
  • Propose something else. A translation of a story. An in class performance of a story. A video.

All Projects are graded in four parts.

Part 1. Proposal (5 points)

The proposal will consist of a one page document laying out which option, which shorts, and which critical or theoretical readings from the syllabus the student will be working. The proposal draft is due February 24.

Students will then sign-up to meet with Professor Abel in office hours sometime between Februrary 24-March 19 to propose their project.

Final draft of the proposal is two within 48 hours of your conversation with Professor Abel

Part 2. Draft 5 points

The first draft of the proposed project is due April 14.

Part 3. Presentation 5 points

Five to seven minute presentations on the projects are scheduled for the last week of classes. Students will be prepared to present during any of those three class sessions. Presentations will be evaluated on three categories: clarity of presentation, quality of project, and the degree to which you were able to link your presentation to our course discussions.

Part 4. Final 5 points

The final draft is due on the Monday of exam week, Monday May 5. The final will be evaluated on the degree to which you’ve taken into account the comments of your professor on your draft and the course materials
