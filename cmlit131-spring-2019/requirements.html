Assignments and requirements

Attendance and participation (20 points)

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you will be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply turning up is not enough.

16–20
Frequent, substantial, and consistent verbal participation that demonstrates a good grasp of the reading. This includes answering and asking questions as well as taking part in group activities.
11–15
Occasional, inconsistent, or only superficial verbal participation. This includes demonstrating some grasp of the reading but failing to contribute to class discussion and/or group activities.
1–10
Infrequent, inconsistent, or superficial verbal participation. This includes rarely if ever answering or asking questions, failure to grasp the reading or participate in group activities.
0
Very poor or no participation; weak or no grasp of the readings; disruptive, disengaged, or uncooperative behavior.

You are allowed up to four (4) absences in this course. For every class missed beyond the allowed four, you will lose 5 points from the “Attendance and Participation” portion of your grade.

Reading response worksheets (15 points)

These are short, written exercises in which you briefly respond to the reading assigned for the assignment before it is due. You are required to complete fifteen (15) Reading Response Worksheets during the semester; in other words, students are required to submit responses for about half of our roughly 30 sessions. You can choose which days you’d like to submit these assignments. They are not assessed until the completion of the course. But students are encouraged to work towards finishing these assignments early in the semester. Reading response worksheets are available on Canvas and completed forms should be submitted in the appropriate box on Canvas.

Bonus opportunity: Students may submit up to five additional response sheets for a maximum of 5 additional bonus points.

Creative writing assignment (10 points)

This is a class on genre fiction. Genre is both a descriptive and prescriptive mode of understanding literature. As such, the best way to get at a thorough understanding of genre fiction is not only through reading a lot of it, but also through trying our hands and producing some of it. We learn differently when we try making things as opposed to simply analyzing them. These creative writing assignments however are not to gauge your abilities as a creative writer, but rather to gauge your ability to reflect on what you learn from the writing process.

Beginning on the first day of class and continuing throughout the semester, we will be collaborating on updating and maintaining a rulebook for detective fiction based on our readings and discussions. This will guide us as to how close individual work define or fulfill the genre and how much some works push the boundaries of the genre. Active participation in creation and maintenance of our rule book is expected. Students who go the extra mile on this project will be eligible for bonus points (up to 5pts for the semester). Remember google doc tracks all of your edits (including deletions and additions). So be a constructive participant in the forum!

5 points
At the semester’s start (January 10) students will submit a “two minute mystery” of their own creation based on the samples we read in class. Mysteries are to be no longer than 500 words. After the mysteries, students should append a note in italics identifying what they were trying to repeat of the genre as they see it in the examples and what they were trying to add. Appended notes should be no longer than 200 words.
5 points
At the semester’s end (April 25th), students will submit a “two minute mystery” of their own creation based on the samples we read in class. Mysteries are to be no longer than 500 words. After the mysteries, students should append a note in italics identifying what they were trying to repeat of the genre as they see it in the examples and what they were trying to add. In addition, they are to comment on what they learned since they last tried this assignment at the beginning of the semester. Appended notes should be no longer than 400 words.

Criticism presentation (10 points)

All students will sign up for one week to introduce new ideas from critical and theoretical sources. On the week for which they’ve signed up students will coordinate with other students who have signed up for that week to present and lead critical discussion relating the critical work to the literary work for the week. These presentations will happen on Thursdays.

Midterms (30 points)

Mid Term A: Text-based argumentative essay (15 points)

  1. Choose a theme (gender, race, class, history, geography, violence).
  2. Find and identify the clues.
  3. Look for a pattern (three or more appearances of your theme) in one of the class texts on your theme.
  4. Write an argumentative essay suggesting why your theme best elucidates the meaning of the story. Each of your three or more clues must be woven into your argument/thesis in the form of a close reading.

You may only refer to the text as your evidence.

4–7 pages. MLA Format.

Due February 21, 2019

MidTerm B: Contextual research essay (15 points)

  1. Choose three details referred to, mentioned, or suggested by the text that the text alone does not explain.
  2. Research the details outside of your text using library resources and the web.
  3. Write a convincing argument suggesting why your research best elucidates the meaning of the story.

Due April 4, 2019
