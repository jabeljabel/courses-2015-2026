From Mastermind to Paranoiac, From Close Reading to Over-reading, From Text to Context

Course description

This course studies the origins and development of crime and detective literature from an international perspective. Beginning with premodern precursors to the rise of the modern hardboiled novels, the course traces literature’s relations to crime, violence, detection, forensics, and social justice through a variety of historical and cultural contexts.

Some of the questions addressed may include myths about law and order; the rise of urban societies and mass culture; the construction of the detective figure, the witness, the criminal, and the victim as models of subjectivity; issues of class, race, and gender; madness; cultures of sexual violence; and the ideal of justice.

They will develop ideas about the contribution of literary thinking to ideas of social justice, as well as a theory of genre and its development over time; they will recognize regional, cultural, and historical differences and forms of change that affect the intellectual development of literature and detection alike. They will practice and master these skills through class discussion, short papers, regular reading assignments, and longer research papers.

One of the themes we will be tracking in the course is literatures role both in making its readers better readers and potentially over-readers. To what degree are literary detectives masterminds? To what degree are they fallible? When are we reading clues well? When are we over doing it? To what extent is pattern finding and the derivation of theories related to a scientific practice and to what extent is it related to paranoid thinking? These are questions raised by detective fiction that have contemporary relevance to problems with how we understand the world today, be it climate change data, fake news stories, or conspiracy theories.

Course objectives

  • Identify the key components of detective fiction.
  • Discover patterns from clues in literary texts.
  • Analyze patterns and find trends and meanings.
  • Arrange and assemble a logical and compelling argument about a literary text.
  • Develop and demonstrate an intercultural understanding of crime and detective fiction.
  • Describe the world history of crime and detective fiction.
  • Learn to appreciate, discuss, and analyze the ways in which literature responds to and reimagines violence and transgressions of social/legal norms.
  • Learn to effectively communicate observations, opinions, and assertions in writing and speaking about literature
  • Develop and demonstrate the critical thinking, reading, and writing skills essential for further study of world literatures.
