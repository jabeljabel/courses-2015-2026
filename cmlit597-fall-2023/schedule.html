Course schedule

This syllabus is in flux. It is a plan that will be modified as our conversations and interests evolve. Consider the reading list as a directional guide without being a rule. But do complete the readings for week one.

As it currently stands, the schedule was designed to appeal to the broadest possible set of disparate interests.

The readings tend toward the philosophical and abstract rather than criticism per se. The work of seminar will be (in some measure) to fill out the reading list with more practical critical works from our fields. As this is done, other works will be removed so as to maintain a reasonable load.

By the third week of class students will be expected to contribute readings for one week of the syllabus from week three – twelve that relate both to the topic for the week and to their specific fields of interest. The page is editable by registered students. Please edit only after you’ve discussed them with the group in class.

Some readings were added by seminar members.

Unit 1: Digital Materiality

This unit explores the material aspects of digital media, such as the formats, standards, devices, and infrastructures that enable and constrain the production, distribution, and consumption of digital content. We will examine how digital media are shaped by the presence of noise, the inevitability of deterioration, and the processes of compression, pixelization, and archiving, and how these processes impact the aesthetics, politics, and ethics of digital media. We will also consider whether digital humanities methods can or should help us analyze and visualize digital media in new and/or useful ways. We will examine how digital media are shaped by and shape the social, cultural, economic, and environmental contexts in which they operate. We will also consider how digital media challenge and transform our notions of representation, authenticity, preservation, and access. This unit explores the concept of digital materiality and its impact on our understanding of the digital world. It examines the ways in which digital media is constructed and represented, and how it differs from traditional, physical media.

Keywords include: high definition, hi resolution, hi fidelity, lossiness, compression, pixelization, noise, signal, bitrot, archiving, deterioration, and digital humanities.

Unit II: The 2.0-ness of Things: Into Digital Media Ecologies

This unit investigates the social and cultural dimensions of digital media ecologies, such as the platforms, networks, communities, and practices that emerge from and shape the use of digital media since the advent of Web 2.0. We will explore how media are remediated by digital media forms and genres, how they create new forms of participation and engagement, and how they challenge and/or reinforce existing power structures and inequalities. We will also examine how digital media foster “new” modes of expression and creativity, such as memes, fanfiction, fanlation, and remix cultures. As such, the unit delves into the complex interactions between digital media and society.

Keywords include: remediation, skeuomorphic design, inequity of access, platformitivity, social media bubbles, virality, engagement rate.

Unit 3: For Better or for Worse: Everyday Bad Media, Ephemeral Electrons, Low Brow Cultures

This unit examines the ethical and political implications of digital media for individuals and societies, such as the effects of surveillance, big data, addiction, gamification, and disinformation on our identities, relationships, and cultures. We will analyze moral panics around digital media; they are thought to: be haunting (think Dark Mirror or Japanese horror movies); create and foment deep ideological biases, and harbor hidden agendas (to addict, to measure, to lead opinion). We will also examine how they create new forms of resistance, activism, and empowerment. Finally, we will reflect on our own roles and responsibilities as digital media users and scholars.

Keywords include: haunted media, moral panic, surveillance, big data, mind mush, asociality, children, internet addition, gaming addiction, daemons, algorithms, AI, gamification of everyday life, digitalization, QR coding, geotagging, biometrics, vr, and ar.

Unit 4: Postmodern, Posttruth, Postmedia Environments

This unit explores the emerging trends and challenges of digital media in the so-called post-truth era, such as the proliferation of disinformation and misinformation, the rise of analogue nostalgia and off-the-grid fictions, and the growing desire for digital media literacy and detoxification. We will discuss how digital media are transforming social notions of truth, reality, and authenticity, and how they are affecting our emotional and mental well-being. We will also consider how we can develop critical skills and strategies to cope.

Keywords include: disinformation; misinformation; postmodernism; post-truth; analogue nostalgia; off the grid; social media detox; fake news.
