Course description

Despite the supposed failures of Communism and Socialism, the influence of the philosopher, economist, historian, and librarian Karl Marx continues to reverberate today in the fields of economics, history, political science, as well as cultural criticism and critical theory. This seminar surveys Marxist literary and cultural criticism with an emphasis placed on the critical theoretical traditions that have drawn inspiration from Marxist thought.

This course is not a review of the record of Marxist thought in practice outside of cultural study (its successes or failures), but rather takes for granted the fact that Marxist thought as a critical practice already underpins, overlaps with, provides a reference point for, or in some way touches upon most of the humanist thought done globally today. As such, this is also not a course on Marxists from around the world (though many of the authors to be discussed considered themselves Marxists), but rather a course that reads thought in a Marxist vein. Much of the work of the seminar will, therefore, be taken up with establishing and identifying such questions of connection and dispersal, evolution and revolution. Beginning with some of the more cited essays and writing by Marx that deal directly with cultural production, circulation, and reception, the course quickly moves to literary criticism and theory overtly and implicitly inspired by Marxist thought from around the world.

Topics and keywords may include: abstraction, alienation, appropriation, commodity fetish theory, conversion, cultural materialism, dialectical materialism, extraction, fellow traveler, Frankfurt School, labor, materialist feminism, object oriented ontology, sociocriticism, structuralist Marxism, tendency, value, Western Marxism, work.

Recommended texts

It is recommended that students acquire Jeff Diamanti’s Bloomsbury Companion to Marx as well as Nelson and Grossberg’s Marxism and the Interpretation of Culture for this course as they provide not only several of the readings for the course but great references and sources for further reading. Of course, Terry Eagleton’s books may also prove helpful in this regard.
