Participation

Class attendance and participation (20%)

A significant portion of the grade for this course is directly tied to your participation in this class. Successful participation is defined as consistently adhering to University requirements in addition to those outlined here. Participation includes engaging in class, group, or other activities that solicit your feedback on the readings or material.

Students are expected to complete all of the reading for the week in time for class. Students are expected to attend all class meetings. If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you should be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply turning up is not enough.

Regular attendance is required. You are allowed six absences free of consequence. Additional unexcused absences will result in your class participation grade being lowered a letter grade for each additional absence. After nine unexcused absences, you will fail the course. Late work will not be accepted in cases of unexcused absences. You are expected to arrive on time for class; recurrent tardiness may result in a recorded absence. Failing to complete reading or do other course preparation for class meeting will also be recorded as a participation failure for the day.

To be counted as present for attendance, you must be prepared and participate in that class session. Preparation of class materials and the frequency and quality of participation in discussions is part of showing up.

At two points (weeks 5, 10) in the semester, students will submit self evaluations of their attendance and participation. When they have done so, I will respond with a post mid-term evaluations of each student’s participation and attendance.

10 course material write-ups (10%)

Students will be expected to complete 10 course material write-ups on primary materials by the end of the semester. The write-ups should be done directly posted to this canvas box as a file or as text, whichever is easier.

Write-ups for a given day’s readings should be submitted as on Canvas on the evening prior to the class for which the reading was assigned. (In other words, students are to have finished the primary reading for the Tuesday prior to class by Monday at midnight.) The point of this is to begin a conversation that can be continued in class.

The Write-ups are opportunities for you to display what you have garnered from the readings and to influence class discussions. Students who have difficulty speaking in class are particularly encouraged to take this assignment seriously as it will prepare you to be more outspoken in class and show your classmates and instructor that you have important ideas.

Your responses in the write-ups will often be shared with the entire class and students should not consider them confidential work for the eyes of the professor only.

Make sure your response includes:

  • Title and Year of the work
  • Keywords: Write 3-5 keywords for the book and/or readings and note the scenes and pages on which these words appear in an important context. Describe how you think the words you have chosen are essential to the text.
  • Copy three short quotations that are your favorite passages. Be prepared to discuss why you chose them. (be sure to note the page numbers)
  • Analysis: Provide an analysis of two to three major arguments, themes, or moods in the text.
  • Questions: Write three questions about the work that can serve as prompt for class discussion. It helps to focus on a particular passage (make sure to include the page number). Please ensure that the questions are sophisticated enough to ignite interest discussion about the text.

In-class presentation (10%)

Presentations are your opportunity to pursue your interests that may differ from or be under-covered in the subjects of the class syllabus.

The presentation will consist of a small research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some historical, material, financial, geographical, biological, biographical, or other contexts to a given week’s text or on topics which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings. Handouts or slides (consisting of visual aids, quotations, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required. You must submit to canvas a copy of your slides and/or handouts the day before your presentation.

Presentations should not exceed ten minutes in length. Presentations will generally happen on Tuesday’s class.

You will not be permitted to present in class if you have not discussed your presentation with me. All presenters should plan a meeting with me in office hours to discuss their topic at least one week prior to their presentation. Don’t risk getting a zero for the assignment. The meeting is to facilitate you arriving at a topic that is both of interest to you and beneficial to the class.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

Rubric: Presentations will be evaluated in three categories:

  1. Contribution to our ongoing discussions in class. This means your presentation should not be a complete digression from our discussions but should relate your specific interests in the text of the week to our on going collective points of interest.
  2. Quality of outside research. This is an assessment of the depth of research into your chosen context beyond the assigned text that you’ve displayed and made relevant for interpreting the text at hand.
  3. Quality of interaction and engagement with your classmates. This will assess the way you’ve brought the class into discussion of your presentation’s argument or point. You can ask questions, do polls, do breakout rooms or whatever else you think will focus your classmates on your topic. For instance, one easy way to do this is to send everyone to breakout rooms to come up with more evidence to support your presentation or more to refute it.

Written work

Making: Special project (15%)

This project is to be decided by the student and approved by professor Abel. The idea behind it is to allow students to think beyond the scope of the traditional research paper. Possible successful projects examples include retelling a fairy tales in graphic novel format, translating scenes from an as yet untranslated graphic novel, reframing stories we have read in class using alternative different images (from your own design or taken from elsewhere), and tellings of new stories using a mash-up of images from graphic novels in our course. But the possibilities are limited only by your imagination and by your ability to convince professor Abel that this is a worthwhile, stimulating, and intellectual endeavor that will get you thinking about graphic novels in a way which you would otherwise have been unable to achieve.

Make sure to clear your project with Professor Abel in office hours as a proposal by email or meeting in office hours before you start work.

You will present your special project to the class twice.

First, as an idea presentation with the class. For your idea presentation you will explain the project to your classmates, show them what you’ve done so far (so they can have an idea of the project), and ask for advice on how to finish and make the project better.

Second, everyone will present their final product in week 15. Your project must be complete by and submitted in digital form to this canvas site by April 23. Be ready to present on either the Tuesday or Thursday of that week (you will be called randomly).

This very short final presentation should try to answer the question: what have I learned from doing this project (making something/using the creative faculties/performing this thought experiment) which I might not have learned (or could only have learned in a less direct way) from doing a standard research paper?

All students will be assigned anonymous peer reviews of the project to be completed after the last presentations on April 29.

Rubric: There are five categories for the evaluation of the project:

  1. the project concept
  2. the project explanation of the concept in your pre and post project presentations
  3. the execution of the project: (how well you were able to achieve concept)
  4. the execution of the project: (how well you were able to explain what remains to be done or was not well achieved)
  5. your final presentation of the take home message (what I learned from the project that would have been difficult or impossible from a research paper).

These five categories are given roughly equal weight (so you can think of them each as about 3 points which yields a total of (3X5=) 15 points.

Research

Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing. Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one literary work discussed in class, a discussion of how the work relates to several of the course readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class. All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) style format. Students are encouraged to use academic journals, books published by university presses, and primary sources as references for their research.

Mid-term paper (20%)

The finished product should be between three and five pages (excluding bibliography in MLA format).

Due Thursday, March 4, 2021

Have a look at the assignment guide (PDF).

Final paper (25%)

This is a comparative paper that considers two or more primary sources (graphic novels) in their contexts. These research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one literary work discussed in class, a discussion of how the work relates to several of the course readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class.

Have a look at the mid term assignment guide. The tasks are roughly the same. But now you have also set up a comparison. What do your two comics share? How do they differ? Are you situating the two texts within the same context or two different contexts? Is your paper going to largely be a continuity and similarity argument or a difference and discontinuity argument? What does the comparison of the two texts in contexts get you that you might not have got with studying only one of the texts?

The finished product should be between seven and ten pages (excluding bibliography in MLA format).

Due Monday of exam week (May 3, 2021)

Note on options (added April 9)

Students can either continue with the course as planned. Let’s call this OPTION A. For OPTION A the grading breakdown is the same as has been listed on syllabus since the beginning of the semester: Special Project 15%, Mid Term Paper 20%, Final Paper 25% (subtotal 60%).

OR choose OPTION B (a new grading breakdown that would apply your average for your Special Project and MidTerm grades as your grade for the final): Special Project 15%, Mid Term Paper 20%, FINAL = Average of Special Project and MidTerm 25% (subtotal 60%).

FOR EXAMPLE: Under OPTION B: if you receive 13/15 for your special project and 18/20 on your mid term for a combined 31/35 points for those two assignments. You’d have an 88% (=31÷35) average on the two written assignments. This average of .88 would be applied to the 25points left for the final (.88*25=22). So for the total of 60 points for the Written Assignments section of the course, you’d have 31+22= 53/60.

My recommendations would be as follows: OPTION A would make sense for people who have a good start on their final paper and are confident that they can do better on their final paper than on the mid-term and special project to bring their grade up. OPTION B would make sense for people feeling the crunch of the semester upon them and feel like they have already achieved their full potential in the class.

Please note that I’d be happy to go over grade calculations in my office hours to help you gauge what option to take, though I think the calculation is one you can easily make using my example above as a base.

You do not have to let me know which OPTION you take prior to exam week. I will simply assume you are doing OPTION A if you submit a final on Monday of exam week; and I will assume you are taking OPTION B if you DO NOT submit a final.

I would not recommend using the ones that offer to convert your photo since we don’t know what they will do with your face data.

This assignment is a bonus five points! If you get creative and detailed with your “iconic me” or do two “iconic me”s, you may receive even more bonus points.
