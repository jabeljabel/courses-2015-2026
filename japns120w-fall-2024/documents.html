Short critical bibliography

The following is short list of critical texts from which Thursday critical presentations may come. You may of course choose other texts with the approval of Dr. Abel (note they should be peer reviewed critical pieces).

  • Beichman, Janine. 2002. Embracing the Firebird: Yosano Akiko and the Birth of the Female Voice in Modern Japanese Poetry. University of Hawaii Press.
  • Benjamin, Walter. 2002. “Mapping the Space of Mass Culture: Kawabata Yasunari’s.” Topographies of Japanese Modernism, 117.
  • Bernstein, Gail Lee, ed. 2019. “EIGHT. Yosano Akiko and the Taishō Debate over the ‘New Woman’.” In Recreating Japanese Women, 1600-1945, 175–98. University of California Press. https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520910188-011.
  • Carnes, Pack. 1992. “The Japanese Face of Aesop: Hoshi Shin’ichi and Modern Fable Tradition.” Journal of Folklore Research, 1–22.
  • “Chapter 7. The Limits of Language The Poetry of Tanikawa Shuntarò.” 2017. In Modernism in Practice, by Leith Morton, 158–78. University of Hawaii Press. https://doi.org/10.1515/9780824844561-009.
  • Clements, Jacob. 2019. “Murakami Haruki’s Short Fiction and the Japanese Consumer Society.” PhD Thesis, University of Kansas. https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/handle/1808/30078.
  • Cornyetz, Nina, and Rebecca Copeland. n.d. “Reading Desire in a New Generation of Japanese Women Writers.” Accessed August 3, 2024. https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003368632.
  • Delfrati, Sabina. 1994. “Silence and Word. The Language of Images in Kawabata Yasunari’s.” https://www.tesionline.it/tesi/lingue-e-letterature-straniere/silence-and-word-the-language-of-images-in-kawabata-yasunari-s-/33994.
  • Dorsey, James. 2001. “Culture, Nationalism, and Sakaguchi Ango.” Journal of Japanese Studies, 347–79. https://doi.org/10.2307/3591970.
  • Dorsey, James, and Douglas Slaymaker. 2010. Literary Mischief: Sakaguchi Ango, Culture, and the War. Lexington Books.
  • Ericson, Joan E. 1997. Be a Woman: Hayashi Fumiko and Modern Japanese Women’s Literature. University of Hawaii Press.
  • Gerlach, Hayley. 2024. “Ecology, Society, and Imagination in Oyamada Hiroko’s The Factory and The Hole.” PhD Thesis, University of Pittsburgh. http://d-scholarship.pitt.edu/46035/.
  • Gerow, Aaron. 2018. “Kawabata and Cinema: The Ambivalence of Knowledge, Medium, and Influence.” Japan Forum 30 (1): 26–41. https://doi.org/10.1080/09555803.2017.1307254.
  • Goyet, Florence. 2000. “Akutagawa and the’Western’Short Story.” Short Story Criticism 44.
  • Haslam, Jeffrey. 2022. “A Social Disruption: The Decentering of the Individual in Contemporary Dystopian Fiction and Its Challenges to Humanism, Posthumanism, and Neoliberal Individualism.” https://escholarship.org/uc/item/10s1v91k.
  • Hetrick, Jay. 2024. “Toward a Critical Non-Humanism in Postwar Japan.” Asian Studies 12 (2): 309–41. https://doi.org/10.4312/as.2024.12.2.309-341.
  • Huang, Yingcheng. 2022. “Haruki Murakami and His Realistic Style: The Japanese Aesthetic Taste in Norwegian Wood.” In 2022 3rd International Conference on Language, Art and Cultural Exchange (ICLACE 2022), 773–79. Atlantis Press.
  • Inouye, Charles Shirō. 1998a. “Embraces 1897–1899.” In The Similitude of Blossoms, 119–40. Harvard University Asia Center.
  • ———. 1998b. “Language, Love, Death.” In The Similitude of Blossoms, 156–78. Harvard University Asia Center.
  • Larson, Phyllis Hyland. 1991. “Yosano Akiko and the Re-Creation of the Female Self: An Autogynography.” The Journal of the Association of Teachers of Japanese 25 (1): 11–26. https://doi.org/10.2307/488908.
  • Lippit, Miya Mizuta. 2002. “Reconfiguring Visuality: Literary Realism and Illustration in Meiji Japan.” Review of Japanese Culture and Society 14:9–24.
  • Morton, Leith. 1998. “An Interview with Shuntaro Tanikawa.” Southerly 58 (1): 6–30.
  • Oda, Tomoko. 2018. “On Pre-Translatability: The Case of Two Fantastic Short Stories by Haruki Murakami.” Creativity 1 (1): 89–106. https://doi.org/10.22381/C1120183.
  • Peterson, James. 1989. “A War of Utter Rebellion: Kinugasa’s ‘Page of Madness’ and the Japanese Avant-Garde of the 1920s.” Cinema Journal 29 (1): 36–53. https://doi.org/10.2307/1225300.
  • Pulvers, Roger. 2023. “Betsuyaku Minoru: The Playwright Who Came Back to a Place He Had Never Been To.” Asian Theatre Journal 40 (2): 291–305. https://doi.org/10.1353/atj.2023.a912917.
  • Rabson, Steve. 1991. “Yosano Akiko on War: To Give One’s Life or Not: A Question of Which War.” The Journal of the Association of Teachers of Japanese 25 (1): 45–74. https://doi.org/10.2307/488910.
  • Takahashi, Yasunari. 1994. “Tradition and Experiment in Contemporary Japanese Theatre.” Contemporary Theatre Review 1 (2): 105–11. https://doi.org/10.1080/10486809408568269.
  • Tanaka, Mariko Hori. 2014. “THEATRE IN THE 1960s.” Edinburgh Companion to Samuel Beckett and the Arts, 465.
  • Tillack, Peter. 2024. “Writing a Place for Politics in the Space of Capital: Oyamada Hiroko’s Kōjō (The Factory, 2013).” In Reading Desire in a New Generation of Japanese Women Writers, 106–27. Routledge. https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9781003368632-7/writing-place-politics-space-capital-peter-tillack.
  • Tommasi, Francesco, Johanna L. Degen, and Matthijs P. Bal. 2022. “The Use of Fiction as a Distinctively Critical Research Approach to Debunk Work and Organizational Psychology Constructs: Initial Thoughts.” In Booklet, 1–1. https://iris.univr.it/handle/11562/1074506.
  • Wu, Fengjuan. 2017. “Research on Personalities of Characters in Ryunosuke Akutagawa’s Early Works–Rashomon, The Nose and Steal Taken as Examples.” In 2017 International Conference on Culture, Education and Financial Development of Modern Society (ICCESE 2017), 299–302. Atlantis Press. https://doi.org/10.2991/iccese-17.2017.77.
  • Yamano, Koichi, Kazuko Behrens, Darko Suvin, and Takayuki Tatsumi. 1994. “Japanese sF, Its Originality and Orientation (1969).” Science Fiction Studies, 67–80.
  • Yi, Yongfei. 2011. “Sakaguchi Ango’s Conceptualizations of the Function of Literature in the Postwar Era.” Master’s Thesis, The Ohio State University. https://rave.ohiolink.edu/etdc/view?acc_num=osu1313435605.
  • Yosano Akiko. 2001. Travels in Manchuria and Mongolia: A Feminist Poet from Japan Encounters Prewar China. Columbia University Press. https://doi.org/10.7312/yosa12318.
