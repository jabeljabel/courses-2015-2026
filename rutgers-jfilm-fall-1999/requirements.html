Participation

Class attendance and participation

Students are expected to attend all class meetings and screenings. Students are expected to complete all of the reading for the week in time for the Thursday evening lecture and discussion session.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

Film discussions bulletin board

Students will be expected to participate in an electronic newsgroup (bulletin board), discussing their thoughts on the films, readings, and class discussions. Topics and the manner of the discussion will be decided by discussion leaders. Each and every student will take a turn being a discussion leader. You will have free rein here to discuss topics that do not get raised in class or in the readings. This is a chance to find out what “the audience” thinks. Also these discussions can form the basis for ideas for the Film Journal/Thought Papers.

Discussion leaders must write and send at least two paragraphs (10 sentences) by Wednesday at midnight.

Discussants (that is everyone else) must write at least one response by the following Monday at midnight. Responses may be any length three sentences or over. They can respond to any one or all of the discussion leaders for a given week.

All students must sign up for a week to be a discussion leader by the third week of class.

Group project

The group project will consist of a collective research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. Groups shall be formed of no less than two and no more than four students. The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on topics in Japanese film which may be confusing and/or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class or by readings. Handouts (consisting of visual aids, definitions of terms, and annotated bibliographies) are required.

All students must sign-up by the third week of class on the sign up sheet for a date of presentation and a topic. All groups should plan a meeting with me at least one week prior to their presentation.

Quiz

An online, open-book quiz will be given in the fourth week of class. Students can take the quiz anytime between Tuesday and Friday of that week. Students will have one hour to complete multiple-choice, short answer, and short essay questions on film terms.

Written work

Film journal/ thought papers

Short (one- or two- pages) reports on each film viewed in class will be due at the beginning of following week’s film. Entries must note the following:

  • Film name and year of release
  • Director’s name
  • Production company name (when available)
  • Setting
  • Main characters’ names and brief descriptions
  • Plot summary
  • Themes, motifs, recurring images
  • Notable scenes
  • Most importantly, thoughts on the film (between five and fifteen sentences.) These can be comments, criticisms, or questions. They should relate the film to class discussion, newsgroup discussion, and/or the readings.

Final papers

Final papers are individual labors. Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing. Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one film, a discussion of how the film relates to several of the course readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class. The finished product should be between five and seven pages (excluding bibliography). All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) format.

Due Thursday, December 9
