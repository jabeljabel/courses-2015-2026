Course description

Today climate change effects every organism on the planet. Although the role of the human causing planetary changes to our climate has long been proven by scientific analysis of geological and climate data, the cultural and ideological roles enabling, justifying, forming, and transforming the human decision-making responsible for those changes remains under-interrogated. This seminar surveys the recent rise of ecocriticism in cultural studies and its antecedents.

This course is not a review of the record of policy debates around climate change, but rather takes for granted the scientific fact that climate change is impacting life on the planet to examine the role of global cultures in that change. The seminar examines the role of culture in grounding and informing science, public policy, environmental activism, and ecological social justice even as culture itself is also impacted by such endeavors.

The course will examine a range ecocritical approaches to culture. Topics covered by the seminar will be partially determined by the participants, but will likely include: ecofeminism, cultural histories of the human relationship with the environment, cli-fi, ecopoetry, environmental racism, environmental classicism, nature writing, toxic consciousness, ecocentricism, anthropocene, romanticism, life writing, ecosophy, ecocinema, Zoocriticism.

Texts

These books you will need to acquire on your own from your favorite library or bookseller:

  • Marran, Christine L., Ecology Without Culture: Aesthetics for a Toxic World. 3rd edition, Univ of Minnesota Pr, 2017.
  • Bruyere, Vincent. Perishability Fatigue: Forays Into Environmental Loss and Decay. Columbia University Press, 2018.

It is recommended that students refer to the following texts for background:

  • The Ecocriticism Reader: Landmarks in Literary Ecology. eds. Cheryll Glotfelty and Harold Fromm. University of Georgia Press (1996).
  • The Oxford Handbook of Ecocriticism. ed. Greg Garrard. Oxford University Press, 2014.
  • Routledge Handbook of Ecocriticism and Environmental Communication. Eds. Slovic, Rangarajan, Sarveswaran, Taylor & Francis, 2019.
  • New International Voices in Ecocriticism. ed. Oppermann. Lexington Books, 2014.

They provide not only several of the readings for the course, but great references and sources for further reading.
