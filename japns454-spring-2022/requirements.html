Evaluation guidelines for attendance & participation

90–100%
Student is well prepared, as demonstrated by her/his critical response papers and comments made in class; is interested in and respects others’ opinions; asks and responds to questions; demonstrates knowledge of course materials; fosters a vibrant intellectual community.
80–89%
Student is generally prepared, makes an effort to refer to readings and course topics; is willing to participate in discussions in class.

Coursework requirements

Attendance and participation (30%)

Attendance

Attendance is the easiest portion of your grade. Show up with your materials and you get points. Students are expected to attend all class meetings. If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding this requirements, that student should contact me immediately. In order to receive credit for attendance, you must not only be present in class, but also bring marked copies of the readings to class and be prepared to give your opinions and questions on the reading. You must also bring any written material assigned for that class meeting.

Participation

You will not be given participation points if you don’t show up having attempted the readings and brought questions and concerns into the classroom discussion.

You are responsible for completing a given day’s readings prior to class. The instructor reserves the right to give pop quizzes on the readings if students seem not to be keeping up with the assignments.

All readings appear in our texts or will be provided via links from the course website. Students should bring copies of readings to class.

In order to receive credit for in-class discussion, you must email me a self-assessment report in English. Every month students must email me a self-evaluation report assessing their own in-class performance. The self-assessment should include the words “self-assessment” in the subject header and a letter grade of A, B, C, D, or F. Due dates are posted on the schedule. See Evaluation Guidelines for Attendance & Participation to aide your self-assessment. I will respond via email to your emailed assessment with my evaluation of your performance. The purpose of the self evaluation is to allow us both to have a forthright perception of performance in class.

Pop quizzes (10%)

If students are not coming to class prepared to participate, there may be pop quizzes that factor into the grade for preparation. There will be one scheduled quizzes on the Japanese Literary Terms. The total value of all quizzes will be 10% of the final grade or 1/3 of the attendance and preparation grade.

Short assignments (10%)

5 short homework assignments based on readings, discussions, and your research. The 5 homework will be assigned on Thursday classes meetings to be due on the following Tuesday meeting. See individual assignments for instructions.

In-class individual presentation (10%)

The presentation will consist of a research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. One purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some contexts to a given week’s reading which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially. Another is to introduce new vocabulary and grammar to students that relates to the content of the presentation. Students will also make interpretive readings of particular scenes from stories during their presentations that relate the context to the story. As a companion to the presentations, all students must plan a meeting with me in office hours to discuss their plan and work at least two weeks prior to their presentation.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

For more detail, see presentation guidelines.

At home project (20%)

Students will sign up for a literature project by week three. All projects must be approved by Professor Abel one week prior to presenting your project to the class. The subject of the project is open to the students (and can be a group project), but it must involve the use of your language skills to make sense of a literary text. For example, possible projects include a short translation of a literary work that has yet to be translated, the performance of a scene in a novel or drama, or subtitling an adaptation of a literary work. The project involves at least two components: a lasting tangible memory of your project (this can be a translation, vocabulary list, video file); and the in class presentation of the project to your peers. This at home project presentation should not be confused with the individual presentation discussed above.

Due May 2 (draft due Apr 21)

Literary terms quizzes (10%)

Early in the semester we will have four short literary terms quizzes to help you learn the vocabulary you need to discuss literature in Japanese.

One class translation project (10%)

As a group, the students will choose a reading for the syllabus that has never been translated into English and work as a group to translate it.

5 reading journals (10%)

Over the course of the semester you will be responsible for turning in 5 reading journals of 250-500 characters each. You may choose which week you would like to do a journal for, but they must be submitted within one week from the reading’s appearance on the schedule. Journals should summarize the main plot and give your analysis of the importance of one scene or image in particular. These may be typed. And should be submitted on canvas for the week in which the given reading is due.
