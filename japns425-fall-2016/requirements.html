Participation in the seminar

Attendance and participation (20%)

Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Students are expected to complete all of the readings listed for the day in time for the beginning of class.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

Regular attendance is required. You are allowed five absences free of consequence. Additional unexcused absences will result in your class participation grade being lowered a letter grade for each additional absence. After nine unexcused absences, you will fail the course. Late work will not be accepted in cases of unexcused absences. You are expected to arrive on time for class; recurrent tardiness may result in a recorded absence. Failing to complete reading or do other course preparation for class meeting will also be recorded as a participation failure for the day.

Participation

Productive participation is qualitative, but comes down to preparing the assigned readings on time to discuss them in class, and then contributing to discussion. Discussion is more than giving your input, and includes responding to other speakers. Helping another student develop his/her ideas by making useful suggestions or asking questions can be the most productive form of participation.

In the jargon of educational theory, this course is both “student centered” and “learning centered.” That is, it shifts the focus away from a model of knowledge imparted by professors (although inevitably there will be some of this), and toward analysis generated by the students. Research shows that students learn more – in terms of both skills and content -- and retain more from this model of education. Unsurprisingly, this model requires a greater commitment of time and energy from students. Enrollment in this class constitutes a commitment to put in that energy and to learn from one another.

To receive credit for participation you must be prepared for class. Preparation is more than simply reading: take notes on the reading to prepare for discussion: write down questions you have about the texts, as well as points you think are interesting and things you disagree with. Contribute your thoughts to discussion. Students must bring a copy of assigned readings to class so we can refer to specific parts of the text: books, print-out, a tablet or laptop are all ways to do this. You should also have written notes, questions, and comments on your reading to prepare for discussion.

Daily assignment: Reading Checksheet (10%)

The Reading Checksheets are opportunities for you to display what you have garnered from the readings and to influence class discussions. Students who have difficulty speaking in class are particularly encouraged to take this assignment seriously as it will prepare you to be more outspoken in class and show your classmates and instructor that you have important ideas.

Material in the write-ups will be shared with the entire class and students should not consider them confidential work for the eyes of the professor only.

Students will be expected to complete 15 Reading Response sheets on reading materials by the end of the semester. The form is available on our website.

Forms on a given class’s readings should be submitted digitally by the midnight before the class’s meeting for which the reading is due (for example, submit your Reading Response sheet for Tuesday’s class on Monday at midnight to the dropbox on Angel.)

Presentation Project (10%)

The presentation will consist of a small (outside and beyond given course materials) research project and in-class oral/visual presentation. The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some historical, material, financial, geographical, biological, biographical, cultural or other contexts to a given week’s text or artistic media which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class discussions or readings. Handouts or slides (consisting of visual aids, quotations, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required. You must submit to professor Abel a copy of your slides and/or handouts.

Presentations should not exceed ten minutes in length. Presentations will generally happen on Monday’s class.

All presenters should plan a meeting with me in office hours to discuss their topic at least one week prior to their presentation. The meeting is to facilitate you arriving at a topic that is both of interest to you and beneficial to the class.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

Written assignments

Special Project (15%)

This project is to be decided by the student and approved by professor Abel. The idea behind it is to allow students use their creative abilities to think beyond the scope of the traditional research paper. Possible successful projects examples include designing a series of kimono patterns, retelling Japanese fairy tales in manga format, distilling a lengthy manga into a poster of your design, creating a video of scene from one of the things we’ve read, or reframing stories we have read in class using alternative different images (from your own design or taken from elsewhere). But the possibilities are limited only by your imagination and by your ability to convince professor Abel that this is a worthwhile, stimulating, and intellectual endeavor that will get you thinking about Japanese visual cultures in a way which you would otherwise have been unable to achieve.

SPECIAL PROJECTS will be presented on Thursdays. Presentations should include reflection on your process of creation. Presentations should address what was learned through the process of making that could not have been achieved through simply reading about the medium. You need to submit a lasting version of your presentation to be evaluated on the day of your presentation (this can be in the form of a link to a website of your design, a printed handout, a powerpoint file, or whatever means you think appropriate to the project that best represents it).

Sign up for your date by the end of September. Your project must be complete by the day you present it. Make sure to clear your topic with Professor Abel in office hours at least two weeks prior to presenting your special project to the class.

Research

Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing. Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one visual and one textual work discussed in class, a discussion of how the works relate to several of the course themes and readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class. All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) style format. Students are encouraged to use academic journals, books published by university presses, and primary sources as references for their research.

The most important quality of each paper is that it be organized around a clear thesis. This means 1) papers must have an introduction stating the thesis, 2) every paragraph must have a topic sentence connected to the thesis, and 3) the conclusion must assert the significance of the thesis. Check each paper to make sure it does these three things.

Where papers quote readings, these quotations must be cited correctly cite (using complete MLA or Chicago style – no made-up mix’n’match formats!).

Students are strongly encouraged to use the Writing Center (information at www.psu.edu/dept/cew/) and to consult with the professor while preparing their papers.

Bonus opportunities

In any semester, each and everyone of us may have issues arise that prevent us from working at the top of our abilities. The evaluation of your progress in this course acknowledges that occasionally life gets in the way of work. To that end, potential optional bonus projects will be announced in class, via email, and/or on the website. In addition, Dr. Abel is willing to consider proposals for potential relevant and appropriate bonus projects (such as writing an extra research paper for 10 points, doing an extra class presentation for up to 10 points, or traveling to a museum and reporting on their Japanese visual art collection up to 10 points).
