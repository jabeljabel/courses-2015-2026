Attendance participation (10 points)

Students are expected to come to class each week having watched two films on the syllabus.

Students who attend class without having prepared by viewing the required films will not be marked as present for a given class.

We will be using polleverywhere in class to foment discussions, so make sure you are registered or you won’t be able to enjoy the fun or be counted as present.

This is a class that reflects on new media. We will use the polling software not only as a gauge of class opinion and record of your attendance and participation, but also as an opportunity to think about how media connect us.

Film responses (10 points)

Students are expected to submit film responses to this dropbox responding to the films on the syllabus that they have watched. Responses are due on the midnight before the day the titles are listed on the syllabus. Students must complete 10 responses by the end of the semester.

Film responses must include the following:

  • Title of the film
  • Year
  • Director
  • Studio
  • Main Actors
  • Five Keywords about the film
  • Three Themes
  • A Short Analysis of what you think the film means or meant for audiences. Including mention of three significant scenes to back up your analysis
  • Three Questions about the film.

Film responses should be no longer than one page single spaced.

Responses forms are available on the canvas website.

They should be filled out and deposited on Canvas for submission. Every form gets submitted to the same dropbox.

Mid term (10 points): time essay

So far we have examined the role of time in understanding a film two ways in this class: first, we thought about a film in terms of its historical context (module 1:timeline); and second, we thought about film in terms of its temporality (module 3: editing/temporality).

For this mid-term paper you must choose a film on our syllabus to read both ways: historically and temporally.

Your thesis should try to answer at least one of the following questions:

  • How does the time in the film (temporality) relate to time out of the film (contextual history)?
  • How does your chosen film organize our viewing time and does this relate to the way historical time contextualizes the film?
  • How is time (editing/narration and history) important to understanding your film?

Papers should be between 5 and 7 pages.

Due Mar 26

Final (10 points): space essay

This is the culmination of your work on CINEmap.

Submit a five page research paper on the meaning of the spaces in one Japanese film.

Drawing on the data you collected, your paper should try to answer ALL of the following questions:

  1. How does film give places meanings?
  2. How do films use the meanings of places?
  3. How did you finding and identify places depicted in the films? What was your process and what were your sources?
  4. How did you research the meanings of those places outside of the films? What was your process for finding information and what were your sources?

Due April 29
