Course description

Senior Seminar in Literary Criticism and Theory · The Pennsylvania State University · Fall 2025

CMLIT 400Y Discussions of theories of literature, of literary criticism, and particularly of the distinct methods of comparative study; individual projects.

What are the underlying structures to our thinking about literary arts? How does understanding the models upon which our interpretations are based help us better understand criticism and the literary or cultural object? Why are so many theories unstated? How are models and theories of cultural products developed and refined? What is their function within criticism.

This course will introduce and examine the most contested field of literary studies, the source of many debates within the humanities, and the butt of far too many jokes in popular media. Although most of us would readily admit that there is likely some model that can ground, order, or explain nearly every phenomenon in the real world including interpretation, explicit articulation of the foundations behind thought about culture has become controversial in recent years, a symbol of all that is wrong with the humanities and higher education. Although theoretical debates have been part of thinking about literary arts since the earliest records of criticism and interpretation, the efficacy of theory itself has recently become a major component of theoretical and notso theoretical inquiry. What are the real world implications of theory? Do they matter or is theory a different order of cognition? Is the activity of theory just naval-gazing for insular ivory tower elites or prodding for world transformation and social engagement?

The course will begin with inquiry into the similarities and differences between interpretation, criticism, and critical theory. Issues of application and practice will focus our attention to the formulation of new models for interpretation and understanding. Topics covered will include: new criticism, narratology, semiotics, author, historicization, Marxism, Feminism, psychoanalysis, reception theory, cultural studies, poststructuralism, and postcolonialism.

We will end with a reflection on the relation of high theory to the culture wars and polarization of the political discourse around cultural study today.

Course objectives

Theories of Objects/Objecting to Theory

  • Understanding role of theory in contemporary criticism.
  • Understanding the basics of contemporary theories.
  • Understanding the fundamental forms of literary comparison.
  • Acquiring proficiency with the language of theory.
  • Gaining insight into uses and abuses of theory.
  • Forming the basis of critical thinking about interpretation.
  • Synthesizing your own interpretive schemata.
  • Understanding the stakes of using particular jargon and avoiding others.
  • Thinking comparatively, critically, theoretically.
  • Understanding the history and geography of ideas and writings now thought of as theory.
  • Reading closely for argument
  • Gain a sense of the variety, breadth and depth of contemporary literary critique.

Some words about reading (theory)

Notes on mastery

When the reading gets tough, the tough read this:

  • There is no starting at the beginning when it comes to theory. So we begin.
  • One cannot master everything. Yet, theory expects you to be or become a master. Master what you can.
  • The more you read the easier reading becomes. It is never easy.
  • Terms and jargon are not for obfuscation but efficiency, elaboration, and explanation.
  • You are expected to have questions and objections to what you read.
  • Using a guidebook, handbook, dictionary, or encyclopedia is not cheating. Every thinker needs help.

Some questions to ask of any interpretation or reading

  • Is it persuasive?
  • Does it tell us something that is not obvious about the text?
  • Does it explain something better than it may otherwise have been explained?
  • Is it dependent on its examples or is exportable?
  • Will it change the way you read?
  • Was it important historically? If so, why?
