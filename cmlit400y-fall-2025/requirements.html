Assignments

Outside Close Reading (30%)

Choose one article or chapter by a theorist on the syllabus. You must have your article approved by the fourth week of class to receive full credit for this assignment. You are to become a master of this text.

You have three one page assignments related to this text (each worth 10% of your grade).

  • Summarize the overall argument and the structure, style. (10Pts) (Oct. 7)
  • Contextualize this article within the career of the author or the field in which the author is writing.(10Pts) (Oct. 28)
  • Give a critique of the article that uses its own logic. Provide evidence drawing on literature to support your claim.(10Pts) (Dec. 2)

Presentation of Outside Reading (15%)

All students will introduce their reading selection to class. Presentations will include any necessary background of the author or historical context in which they wrote. You will finish by setting up pro and con arguments for the book and engaging your fellow students in discussion and/or debate. Outside Reading Presentations (ORPs) will happen on Thursdays.

Once everyone has chosen their outside reading assignment and had it approved, please sign up sheet for a date for your presentation.

Outside Literary Events (15%)

Since learning takes place beyond the confines of the classroom as well as within it, and since it is valuable to practice your critical skills in contexts beyond our assigned readings, 10% of your grade this semester will be associated with your attendance at approved talks about literature that are given by various speakers. You will attend 3 such talks on campus, you will write a brief report (one page) for each one (5%). The reports will consist of an opening paragraph in which you summarize the talk; a second paragraph in which you identify the critical or theoretical assumptions the speaker seems to be using; the final paragraph will be your assessment of whether or not the rubber meets the road and the theory meets, helps to understand the cultural material. These reports are due no later than one week after the talks you attend. Unless you have a documented conflict, the talks you should attend are those in the Department of Comparative Literature’s weekly speaker series, Comparative Literature Luncheon on 12:20-1:20 on Mondays in Kern. Details will be provided on the website, and you can choose any three of these talks; BONUS: for 5% extra credit apiece, you can attend and write a report on even more talks (up to two bonus talks total). However, if you present documentation that you have a conflict on Mondays at that time, you can suggest alternative on-campus talks on literature for approval.

Discussion Board (20%)

Students are expected to participate in an electronic newsgroup (bulletin board), discussing their thoughts on the readings and class discussions. Topics and the manner of the discussion will be decided by DISCUSSION LEADERS. Each and every student will take a turn being a discussion leader. You will have free rein here to discuss topics that do not get raised in class or in the readings. This is a chance to find out what your classmates think about your ideas. It is also a chance for those who are less talkative in class to recover some points for participation.

A given week’s discussion leaders must write and send at least two paragraphs (10 sentences) by FRIDAY at midnight. They should be sure to quote from the following week’s readings. This is a place for provocations, insights, confusions, and questions.

DISCUSSANTS (that is, everyone else) must write at least one response by the following MONDAY, at midnight. Responses may be any length five sentences or over. They can respond to any one or all of the discussion leaders for a given week.

All students must SIGN UP for a week to be a DISCUSSION LEADER by the third week of class.

Preparation, Participation, and Attendance (20%)

Students are expected to attend all class meetings.

Students are expected to complete all of the reading for the week in time for class.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

In order to receive credit for attendance, you must not only be present in class, but also bring marked copies of the readings to class and be prepared to give your opinions on the reading. You must also bring any written material assigned for that class meeting.

In order to receive credit for in-class discussion, you must email me a self-assessment report. Every month students must email me a self-evaluation report assessing their own in-class performance. The self-assessment should include the words “self-assessment” in the subject header and a letter grade of A, B, C, D, or F. Due dates are posted on the schedule.

Thursday readings (weeks 5-10)

Choose your own reading

Since a major argument of this course relates to the infinitude criticism and theory, we will not all always be reading the same things.

The syllabus for weeks 5-12 is organized around major topics or genres or schools of thought.

Students will sign up to read specific readings from our textbook that specifically relate to the topic of the week (see “Alternate Table of Contents” for detailed listings).

Students will then come prepared to discuss those reading with others who have read the same thing in a small group format. They will also present and introduce the readings to the groups that have not read those articles.

There are many purposes of this method for group reading. The major benefit of doing this is so that we may be exposed to the great number of ideas. The risk is that we will sacrifice that which is requisite to theory—deep thinking—for breadth and variety.

At the end of week seven, we will decide as a group whether this format is helping or hurting and decide how to proceed.
