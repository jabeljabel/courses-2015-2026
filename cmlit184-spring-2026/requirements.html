Attendance and Participation (20 points)

This is a discussion-based course; attendance and consistent, active participation are required. This means that you will be on time, prepared (reading done and with you in class), and take part in discussion. Simply being present in class is not enough to count.

16–20
Frequent, substantial, and consistent verbal participation that demonstrates a good grasp of the reading. This includes answering and asking questions as well as taking part in group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are well prepared, as demonstrated by your comments made in class. You are interested in and respect others’ opinions. You complete the readings before class and during class you respond to questions, demonstrate knowledge of course materials, and foster a vibrant intellectual community.
11–15
Occasional, inconsistent, or only superficial verbal participation. This includes demonstrating some grasp of the reading but failing to contribute to class discussion and/or group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are generally prepared, and you make an effort to refer to readings and course topics. You are willing to participate in discussions in class. You might be occassionally distracted but you do not distract others.
1–10
Infrequent, inconsistent, or superficial verbal participation. This includes rarely if ever answering or asking questions, failure to grasp the reading or participate in group activities. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You reluctantly participate in class activities, and you are often unprepared. You only have skimmed the reading. You rarely practice critical thinking and do not demonstrate sufficient knowledge of course materials. You are consistently distracted and/or distract others.
0
Very poor or no participation; weak or no grasp of the readings; disruptive, disengaged, or uncooperative behavior. Check in (in this category the following may apply to you): You are not willing to participate in class activities. You have not done the reading before class. You are unable to ask or answer questions. You are generally unprepared and demonstrate little knowledge of course materials. You are consistently distracted and/or distract others. You are absent (physically or mentally), unprepared, or disruptive in class.

You are allowed up to four (4) absences in this course. For every class missed beyond the allowed four, you will lose 5 points from the “Attendance and Participation” portion of your grade. So at 8 misses you will have a zero for attendance in this class which means the ability to earn no higher than a B- for the class and more than likely only far lower than that.

Making Stories/Telling Stories (10 points)

This is a group activity. During the semester your group will meet in class to create a story inspired by our readings. You will work in class together to write a very short story. At the end of the class workshop session all stories will be submitted. Then a few weeks later your group will present (this can be a reading, performance, slide show or video) your story to the class. All members of the group will receive the same grade for the assignment.

2 MidTerm Exams (each exam is worth 15 points. so a total of 30 points for both exams)

The two mid-term exams are three-part events of 5 points each.

Part 1. Review session: (5 points)

On a designated day before exam week, we will have review sessions. During the review session, the class determines 30 of the most salient terms from the readings and our discussions for the exam. Students also propose and refine potential essay questions that link three or more stories and at least one critical or theoretical work. Students should come with a list of 3-5 terms, concepts, or topics to propose for the identification and significance exam and 1-2 proposals for essay questions to these sessions.

(MidTerm1 will have two review sessions on Tues Feb 17 and Tues Feb 24; MidTerm 2 will have 1 review session on April 9)

Part 2. (5 points)

On Tuesday of exam week, students work individually. They are given a list of 20 of the 30 terms. Students individually must choose 5 (out of 10 downselected for the exam) to write short essays listing the identifications (displaying that they understand the meaning, definitions, contexts, and functions of the terms) and significance (why they are important for the understanding literature, the class, culture, society). Students are allowed to bring a single sheet of notes on US letter size paper to use during the exam.

(MidTerm 1 Day One is Mar 3; MidTerm 2 Day One is Apr 21)

Part 3. (5 points)

Thursday session of exam week is divided into two. For the first half part essay exam, students will work individually. Students are given 2 essay questions from the pool of questions developed in the review session (and later refined by Dr. Abel) from which they must choose to answer one question must choose one of these to answer. For the second half of the session, students will work in their group to discuss the 10 terms left off of Tuesday’s exam. They then choose 4 of these 10 terms in addition to choosing 1 term from the 20 on Tuesday’s exam. They then spend the class session writing up to write up IDs and significances for the 5 terms they have chosen. Every group member of a given group receives the same grade for this assignment.

(MidTerm 1 Day Two is Mar 5; MidTerm 2 Day Two is Apr 23)

Comparative Essay (5 points each/ 10 total)

Two short essays will be written in class this semester to build your skills of close reading, comparing and contrasting, and forming arguments. The assignment will be announced on a Tuesday and you will be given twenty minutes to write on that week’s Thursday. Students may bring a 3x5 index card for notes to the Thursday session. Each writing assignment is worth 5 points.

Final Project (30 points)

The purpose of this final project is to allow students to display a synthesis of what they have learned in this class as well as outside research on their topic. Students can choose from one of two options below or propose their own.

Options

  1. Rework a short story that we have read in light of one or more other short story and one or more critical / theoretical reading. This is a creative endeavor. You can rewrite a story from scratch or play mad-libs with it changing words and phrases in each sentence to create something new and completely different. You can translate a story into a poem or graphic novel. You can create a short video of the story. You can try to do a mashup of several stories. You will then write a reflection of 1-2 pages explaining what you did and why you did it.
  2. Comparative research essay. Choose two or more short stories from class and two or more critical/theoretical pieces that help you understand the stories. Then write a 5-7 page essay arguing why the stories you have chosen are important examples of or aberrations from the works of criticism you have chosen.
  3. Propose something else. A translation of a story. An in class performance of a story.

All Projects are graded in four parts.

Part 1. Proposal (5 points)

The proposal will consist of a one page document laying out which option, which shorts, and which critical or theoretical readings from the syllabus the student will be working. Once a draft of the proposal is complete, students will meet with Dr. Abel to discuss their proposal. Email Dr. Abel to set up a meeting appointment or signup for office hours. After that meeting students should revise their draft according to the discuss and submit the proposal by February 26.

Part 2. Draft 5 points

The first draft of the proposed project is due April 16. The draft should include not only your entire argument, evidence, and argumentation, but also a complete bibliography.

Part 3. Presentation 5 points

Five to seven minute presentations on the projects are scheduled for the last week of classes. Students will be prepared to present during any of those three class sessions. Presentations will be evaluated on three categories: clarity of presentation, quality of project, and the degree to which you were able to link your presentation to our course discussions.

Part 4. Final 15 points

The final draft is due on the Tuesday of exam week, Tuesday May 5. In addition to the standard factors of adherence to the assignment, valid argument, clear argumentation, evidentiary citation, and format, the final will be evaluated on the degree to which you’ve taken into account the comments of your professor on your draft and your fellow students on your professor and the course materials.
